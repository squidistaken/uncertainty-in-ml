\section{Code Repository}
\label{app:code}

The full source code is organised as a Python package under with sub-modules for data loading, preprocessing and feature engineering, model definitions, training and early stopping, and evaluation utilities. The pipeline is exposed through a single command-line entry point with subcommands for each stage: \texttt{download}, \texttt{preprocess}, \texttt{train}, \texttt{evaluate}, and \texttt{tune}. Dependencies are managed with \texttt{uv} and pinned in \texttt{uv.lock} to ensure reproducibility. The fixed random seed of 33 is applied globally at the start of each run.

Figure \ref{fig:error_vs_uncertainty} shows that the LSTM's near-zero correlation ($r = 0.043$) demonstrates that the MC Dropout variance carries no signal about prediction difficulty, as test points cluster at near-zero uncertainty regardless of actual error. The VGP's moderate correlation ($r = 0.344$) is more encouraging, although the predicted standard deviation spans a very narrow absolute range, indicating near-constant posterior variance across inputs.

        \caption{Variational GP}
    \end{minipage}
    \caption{Uncertainty decomposition into total, aleatoric, and epistemic components.}
    \label{fig:disentanglement}
\end{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:disentanglement} shows that for the LSTM, aleatoric uncertainty dominates, and the epistemic component (being the MC Dropout variance) is negligible. For the variational GP, epistemic uncertainty accounts for almost all predictive variance, while the learned noise variance $\sigma_n^2$ is near zero. Neither decomposition is physically plausible for financial returns, which are known to be heteroscedastic.

        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{overleaf/assignment_3/report/results/plots/VariationalGP_error_confidence_threshold.png}
        \caption{Variational GP}
    \end{minipage}
    \caption{RMSE as a function of the percentage of predictions retained, ordered by confidence (lowest uncertainty first).}
    \label{fig:confidence_threshold}
\end{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:confidence_threshold} shows that for the LSTM, RMSE shows no consistent downward trend as predictions are filtered by confidence, remaining roughly flat around 0.0104 throughout. This is consistent with the near-zero error-uncertainty correlation as the MC Dropout uncertainty does not identify the easier predictions. The variational GP shows a different and meaningful pattern, where retaining only the most confident predictions yields an RMSE of approximately 0.0070, compared to 0.0104 when all predictions are included. As more predictions are added, RMSE increases, demonstrating that the uncertainty of the variational GP carries a useful ranking signal: the predictions it is most confident about are genuinely more accurate.
